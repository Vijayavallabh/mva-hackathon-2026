# Challenge the choice of everolimus

19 October 2026. Comparative review aid, not a new drug ranking or treatment plan.
The current decision remains: no rescue-priority drug; everolimus is an optional
mechanistic probe and HCQ is reserve. Phase, useful residual protein, joint-treatment
response and clinical exposure margins remain unresolved.

The most important objection is straightforward: **why investigate this drug before
the strongest competing mechanism?** A familiar approved drug and a large amount of
computation cannot answer it. The following comparison restates existing evidence
under common questions; it is not a head-to-head experiment or a numerical score.

| Route | Favorable evidence retained | What cannot be inferred | Qualification that would change the decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Everolimus / mTORC1 | A different BubR1-mutant mouse context showed increased mTOR-associated signaling. Everolimus has established target pharmacology. | The mouse study did not test rapalog rescue. The signal may be adaptive; the retained human pair is different. Separate drug and deficit profiles do not establish joint benefit. | Demonstrate the proposed pathway state with a useful functional deficit in an appropriate endogenous model; then test drug and deficit together. Failure of a valid prerequisite test stops this branch. |
| NAD/SIRT2 / BUBR1 stability | Genetic SIRT2 overexpression improved survival in a BubR1-hypomorphic mouse context; NMN increased BUBR1 abundance in separate experiments. | Genetic survival, NMN abundance and niacin findings are different interventions and endpoints. They do not establish an approved-drug rescue of this pair. More protein need not mean correctly functioning protein. | Establish whether useful residual BUBR1 is present and whether a specific eligible intervention improves its relevant function without impairing checkpoint timing or useful tissue output. Do not treat precursor compounds as interchangeable. |
| HCQ reserve | Earlier literature contains BUBR1-abundance and aneuploid-stress observations worth retaining. | Abundance is not functional rescue; chromatin-timing effects undermine its use as a selective autophagy control. A different exposure experiment is not a direct comparator. | Reopen only with compound-specific, interpretable flux and chromosome-function evidence in the relevant non-cancer context. |
| Mechanism qualification before any drug | Direct endogenous RNA/protein, restoration and functional measurements can distinguish routes and expose an unsuitable model. | A null result from a failed assay does not establish normality. More characterization alone is not a drug-repurposing result. | Use valid model and assay controls to choose the next intervention or stop a branch. Unknown prerequisites remain unknown rather than receiving favorable default values. |

Sources supporting the route distinctions:

- [Sieben et al., BubR1 allelic effects](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI126863) and
  [corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI144781): different alleles and biological
  context; increased pathway signaling is not proof that suppressing it helps.
- [North et al., SIRT2 and BUBR1](https://doi.org/10.15252/embj.201386907): distinguish
  the genetic survival intervention from the NMN abundance experiments.
- [Wu et al., SIRT2 in a nonprogeroid background](https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.14027):
  no observed lifespan benefit in that different context is not a failed replication
  of rescue in the same BubR1-hypomorphic model.
- [K250 study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2014.09.128): checkpoint behavior and
  BUBR1 abundance require separate measurement.
- [Niacin study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2020.04.008) and
  [methods correction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2020.05.020): neither establishes
  niacin rescue of the retained BUB1B pair.
- The [v37 report](track2-report-v37.md), [full v15 ledger](track2-evidence-v15.json)
  and [v21 amendment](track2-evidence-v21.json) retain the favorable evidence,
  adverse findings, approval details and reasons for every existing disposition.

## Questions for an outside reviewer

1. Is excess mTOR-associated activity with impaired function a defensible first
   qualification question, or does another route have a stronger causal rationale?
2. What single measurement would most efficiently distinguish those routes?
3. Which favorable evidence are we discounting too strongly, and which adverse
   evidence are we overgeneralizing across age, tissue, allele or intervention?
4. What result would make you abandon everolimus even if some proxy improves?
5. Is our proposed functional context feasible, and does its endpoint address the
   biological problem rather than merely providing an easy measurement?

The fair standard is the same for every route: relevant mechanism, interpretable
intervention, useful function, injury, exposure and scope. An alternative's weakness
does not validate everolimus. Conversely, failure of a computational proxy does not
establish universal drug inefficacy. Choosing the first experiment is distinct from
choosing a medicine for a child.
