## Suggested twenty-minute scientific review

Spend the first few minutes reading the brief without our prepared answers. State
the central hypothesis in your own words, then identify the assumption you regard
as most vulnerable. Use the remaining time for these questions:

1. Does the variant-to-mechanism-to-drug argument justify the proposed first
   experiment? Identify the weakest link and the evidence needed to repair it.
2. Is a competing mechanism more persuasive? Name the specific evidence and explain
   what would change priority; please challenge both favorable and adverse claims.
3. Could the proposed measurements confuse growth arrest, cell loss or survivor
   selection with useful functional improvement? Which control or outcome is missing?
4. Do the new corona/MCC/APC/C, mitochondrial integrity and proteostasis readouts
   improve attribution without making the first experiment impractical? Which one
   would you remove or replace, and which threshold would be decisive?
5. Is the proposed non-cancer model and functional readout practical in your area
   of expertise? Identify a feasible first experiment and its decisive negative result.
6. What is the most serious overstatement, omitted source or unnecessary inference?
7. What useful contribution would remain if the drug hypothesis failed?

Please distinguish a demonstrated error from a plausible concern or a question
outside your expertise. Citation or specific report location makes feedback actionable.
There is no need to endorse the entry, forecast a prize or review outside your field.

## Uncoached comprehension and demo check

This can be performed by a different scientific reader who did not develop the
submission. It is formative feedback, not a clinical study or a representative
usability benchmark. Record actual observations, including confusion and noncompletion.

After one uninterrupted pitch viewing, ask the reader to explain:

- What drug is proposed, for what conditional mechanism?
- Which result was actually observed and which example was invented?
- What is the next experiment, and what result would stop it?
- What can another researcher use today?

Then ask the reader to open the demo, inspect the current unmeasured state, select
the recovery-injury example and inspect the all-pass case. Ask them to explain each
decision and download a case record. Do not coach until the first attempt is recorded.
Measure time only for the task actually performed. Failure to understand all-pass
as preclinical review only is a material problem to fix.
