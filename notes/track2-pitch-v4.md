## Narration

### Slide 1 · 0:00–0:30 · Protect normal tissue

A cancer drug can kill a tumour yet harm non-cancer cells that share a genetic
vulnerability. Our Track 2 proposal makes that problem central: preserve normal tissue
before pursuing tumour vulnerability. BUB1B is our genetic starting point, but trans
phase remains unconfirmed. No experiment here demonstrates a treatment benefit.

### Slide 2 · 0:30–1:05 · One conditional experiment

BUB1B encodes BUBR1, which helps control chromosome attachment and separation. We
propose testing everolimus only after confirming excessive mTOR activity in a qualified
model. The motivating mouse study used different alleles and did not test drug rescue.
A randomized rhabdomyosarcoma trial also failed to demonstrate benefit from adding
the related drug temsirolimus. Our hypothesis is conditional downstream functional
improvement, not proven chromosome repair or cancer therapy.

### Slide 3 · 1:05–1:35 · Evidence narrows the choices

The expanded review keeps hydroxychloroquine in reserve. Pralatrexate is a separate
tumour-only possibility for verified fusion-positive models; regression, recurrence
and schedule-dependent toxicity all matter. Other attractive compounds face disputed
mechanisms, tissue-specific harm or unmatched exposure. Entinostat has a Chinese
breast-cancer approval, not a pediatric rhabdomyosarcoma indication. Model summaries
were checked against primary sources, not counted as independent evidence.

### Slide 4 · 1:35–2:20 · Measure useful function and harm

Our proposed experiment starts with independently engineered controls, including cis,
trans and genetic correction. Engineered phase does not resolve the child's phase.
We would qualify the functional defect, measure pathway activity and justify exposure
before screening. Then track accurate division, errors, arrest, death and escape from
mitosis, followed by daughter survival and tissue function. Fewer abnormal surviving
cells alone is not rescue. Randomize treatments, blind scoring and replicate across
clones and days. Stop advancement for unacceptable normal-cell injury, unjustified
exposure or failure to reproduce benefit.

### Slide 5 · 2:20–3:00 · A testable proposal, not a prescription

Our contribution is a reproducible decision framework with explicit reasons to reject
our preferred idea. No drug has established efficacy or a clinical exposure margin
for this pair. AlphaGenome predictions do not resolve those gaps. We disclose OpenAI,
Google DeepMind and Fireworks-hosted GLM use; Fireworks API credits are owner-confirmed,
while training and retention settings remain unverified. We thank the child, family
and organizers. The next scientific step is authorized experimental validation, not
patient dosing. This is research only.

## Expected panel questions

**Why everolimus despite negative tumour evidence?** The declared experiment concerns a
measured downstream functional abnormality, not a claim that the RMS trial was positive.
Without that abnormality or with deficient-normal injury, the hypothesis does not advance.

**Why not more candidates?** Indirect evidence is useful for hypotheses, but more names
do not supply matched exposure or safety. The decision ledger retains rejected alternatives.

**What is actually new?** The proposed genotype-aware integration, normal-cell protection
and complete-fate evaluation—not the discovery of mTOR biology or an established cure.

**Does a perfect Track 1 score validate the treatment?** No. The owner-reported score
does not establish phase, exact functional mechanism or response to any drug.

**Can the proposal proceed without wet-lab results?** It can be submitted honestly as a
research proposal. Advancing toward treatment requires evidence and approvals we do not have.
