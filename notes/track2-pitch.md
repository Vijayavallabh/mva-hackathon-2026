## 0:00–0:30 — the question

Our Track 1 entry recovered the competition's selected BUB1B pair, with owner-reported
scores of one hundred rank points and F-max one. That gives us a starting point for Track
2, not proof that a drug will work. Our own analysis still leaves trans phase unconfirmed.
The next question is harder: can we improve cellular function, or kill a tumour, without
harming non-cancer cells that share the underlying vulnerability?

## 0:30–1:00 — mechanism and uncertainty

BUB1B encodes BUBR1, a protein that helps control chromosome attachment and separation.
We hypothesize impaired function, but have not measured the two alleles' exact effects.
A stop variant may reduce available protein; a missense variant might affect stability
or function. Increasing protein is therefore not enough. We must measure whether cells
actually divide more accurately. We also distinguish developmental tissues from a tumour:
they require different outcomes.

Visual: the two-branch mechanism diagram from the report; no claims of measured pathway activity.

## 1:00–1:35 — two conditional experiments

Our first hypothesis tests everolimus only if the relevant genotype shows excessive mTOR
signalling. The mouse evidence is allele-specific and does not test our exact variant.
Our second tests hydroxychloroquine against tumour lysosomal dependence. The original
aneuploid-cell evidence involved chloroquine, so this is an explicit extrapolation.
Both drugs have approvals elsewhere, but neither has demonstrated benefit in this case.
Each must pass matched non-cancer cell testing and a realistic exposure constraint.

Visual: two candidate cards: hypothesis, prerequisite and stop rule; “preclinical only”.

## 1:35–2:05 — what the skeptical review changed

We retained the negative phase-three temsirolimus trial alongside the encouraging relapse
study. We did not turn mouse longevity into a vitamin recommendation, or a protein-binding
prediction into functional rescue. Ataluren also failed our current EU regulatory gate.
Our twelve-entry ledger records supporting evidence, opposing evidence, safety concerns
and rejection reasons. The strongest alternative explanation is often simple: a drug
makes abnormal cells disappear because it harms them.

Visual: compact “claim → objection → revised decision” examples, with source links.

## 2:05–2:40 — a falsifiable validation path

We propose independent engineered clones, single-allele and cis/trans controls, and precise
genetic correction. Then we measure protein, chromosome segregation and the nominated
pathway before screening drugs. Treatments are randomized within experimental blocks;
images are scored blind. Cells from one well are not independent experiments. We stop
if benefit needs unattainable exposure, disappears on repeat, reflects arrested division,
or harms deficient non-cancer cells as much as the tumour. No such experiments have yet
been performed.

Visual: staged gates, with negative results leading to stop rather than hidden exclusions.

## 2:40–3:00 — contribution and limits

Our contribution is a reproducible way to test a therapeutic window, with an explicit
record of what would falsify each idea. The code, evidence and limitations are public.
There is no treatment recommendation or guaranteed competition outcome. We thank the
child and family, and the Hackathon organizers and partners, for making this research
possible.
