Rehearse once without stopping and record the real finish time. If you run long,
reduce pauses or remove words that repeat a visible label; preserve the synthetic
label, conditional mechanism, lack of wet-lab evidence and preclinical-only boundary.
Do not speed up so much that variant/gene names become unclear. Practice a second
take with natural emphasis. A quiet room and consistent microphone distance matter
more than background music or elaborate transitions.

Pronunciation: BUB1B, “bub one bee”; BUBR1, “bub R one”; mTORC1, “em-tor complex
one”; everolimus, “eh-ver-OH-li-mus”; sirolimus, “sih-ROH-li-mus”; RPE1, “R P E one”;
ProteinMPNN, “protein M P N N”; HeLa, “hee-lah.” Say “around eleven percent” for
the displayed 11.1% synthetic ratio. You need not read DOI links or figure footnotes.

Record at 16:9 with legible slides and clear speech. Prefer a still slide transition
over animations that compete with the explanation. A face camera is optional. A
live demo is unnecessary within three minutes; the deck already shows the decision
logic and the report links the offline tool. Do not open private data, terminals,
credentials or unrelated windows during capture.

After export, watch the entire file. Check audible opening/closing words, slide
alignment, full acknowledgement, legibility and duration. Measure the actual exported
video; teleprompter allocations are not a recording receipt. Add captions corrected
against the actual audio, especially drug/gene names. Use the exact provider disclosure
and acknowledgement in [video description](https://github.com/Vijayavallabh/mva-hackathon-2026/blob/main/notes/track2-video-description-v42.md).
