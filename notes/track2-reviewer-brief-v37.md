# Testing an approved drug against a qualified BUB1B deficit

**Proposal.** Test everolimus, an approved mTORC1 inhibitor, as an optional probe of
useful non-cancer function after establishing a relevant BUB1B deficit and the
proposed pathway abnormality. No drug currently earns rescue priority. The retained
pair's endogenous effects, phase and clinical exposure margins remain unresolved.

**Why this candidate remains testable.** A different BubR1-mutant mouse context
showed increased mTOR-associated signaling, but did not test rapalog rescue.
Everolimus could modify a downstream consequence; it does not replace BUBR1.
Qualify excess mTOR-associated activity with impaired function first. Impaired
dynamic flux remains a separately locked alternative. Neither branch is established. [Mechanism and counterevidence](track2-report-v37.md).

**What weakened the original case.** ProteinMPNN failed the expanded functional
controls. Public expression matching has guide, quality and cell-context limitations;
MTOR agreement does not qualify BUB1B. Functional rapalog findings differ across
studies. The corrected optical comparison rejects a practical neural gain. Full interphase
profiles retain a favorable BUB1B cross-guide signal; mitotic retrieval remains
missing because only one guide qualifies. Plate, aggregation and equal-sample
checks distinguish an assay-development lead from functional validation. These
findings keep the drug claim conditional.

**The experiment that can change the decision.** Establish endogenous protein and
function in single-allele, cis, trans and corrected models, with independent genetic
perturbation/restoration. In a qualified non-cancer context, compare drug plus deficit
with each alone using randomized cultures and blinded scoring. Track every enrolled
cell, accurate viable division, daughter fate, useful lineage function and recovery.
Prespecify follow-up: early relative sensitivity can reverse at later assessment.
Confirm independently with justified benefit, injury and exposure margins.

**How a claim fails.** Invalid assays hold inference; imprecise evidence holds
advancement. A valid interval excluding meaningful benefit stops the tested claim.
Unacceptable injury stops advancement despite an apparent benefit. Passing every
requirement permits preclinical review only. Tumour killing needs its own
deficient-normal controls. No wet-lab experiment has been performed here.

**Reusable output.** The offline Every enrolled cell demo makes the actual HOLD
state and 17 invented evidence cases inspectable without reading code. Its small
kit needs no subject files, GPUs or model weights. A deliberately incomplete
benefit-only shortcut advances 11 examples held or stopped by the full policy; this
is a selected teaching set, not clinical validation. Frozen research retains both
favorable findings and failed controls. A validated assay could improve mechanistic
characterization; a valid negative result could stop an unsupported drug route.
No external adoption or family co-design is claimed.
