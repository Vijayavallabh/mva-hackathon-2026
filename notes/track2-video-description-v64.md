# Track 2 v64: three-minute pitch description

**Title:** Everolimus, conditionally: BUB1B mechanism to a testable drug hypothesis | MVA Hackathon 2026

For this child, there are no registered MVA treatment trials. This three-minute pitch therefore starts one step earlier: qualify endogenous BUB1B function before interpreting any drug. Everolimus is proposed only as a conditional approved-drug hypothesis for further investigation, not as a treatment recommendation.

We characterize the retained BUB1B p.Leu737Ter / p.Asn1002Lys pair at gene level: BUBR1 contributes to spindle-checkpoint and chromosome-attachment control, and biallelic disruption is an established MVA mechanism. The selected pair’s endogenous effect, loss-of-function/gain-of-function direction and phase remain unmeasured, so the first experiment is model qualification rather than drug exposure.

The proposed decision-changing experiment is a four-arm comparison in an endogenously qualified model: deficient + vehicle, deficient + everolimus, corrected + vehicle, corrected + everolimus. Useful output, injury and recovery are measured together, with every founder cell enrolled before exposure. The proposal distinguishes pathway engagement and improved chromosome segregation from growth suppression alone. No candidate has demonstrated functional rescue.

The framework separates a better surviving-cell ratio from genuinely useful cellular output: a synthetic example shows an error ratio improving from 25% to 11.1% while accurate viable output falls from 60/100 to 40/100.

No wet-lab experiment has been performed, no rescue-priority drug is claimed, and no clinical recommendation is made. Any care decision belongs to the child’s family and clinical team.

The offline decision demo and replay scripts verify software behavior only: Python and JavaScript agree on valid teaching cases and reject invalid records. They do not validate biological thresholds, drug efficacy or clinical safety.

Primary evidence highlighted in the pitch includes the carrier-mouse mTORC1 study, the 2010 BUBR1 dysfunction study, the RAPA-EX-01 sirolimus/exercise trial, product-specific Afinitor/Zortress labeling, and the official challenge rules. Full citations, limitations and contrary evidence are in the report.
